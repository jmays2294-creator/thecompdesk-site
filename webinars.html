What your members walk away with

Most injured workers lose ground in the first two weeks — not because their claim was weak, but because nobody told them what the clock was. The session turns vague anxiety into three dates and a short list of things not to do.

  1. What counts as a work injury
  2. Telling your employer — the 30-day notice rule
  3. Filing the claim — and the two-year rule
  4. Medical treatment — who you're allowed to see
  5. Getting paid — average weekly wage and your actual rate
  6. The IME — what it is and how to handle it
  7. Hearings — what actually happens in front of a judge
  8. Better but not the same — schedule loss and classification
  9. Settlement — Section 32, and when it isn't the right call
  10. Retaliation, and how your contract interacts with it
  11. Occupational disease — the claims members never file
  12. The five mistakes that cost members the most

Who presents it

Joel Mays, Esq. is a practicing New York State workers' compensation attorney who appears before the Workers' Compensation Board and handles claims through every stage — from the initial C-3 filing through hearings, permanency, and settlement. He built The Comp Desk because injured workers kept asking him the same questions at hearings.
